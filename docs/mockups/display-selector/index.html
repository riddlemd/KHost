Choosing where the song comes out

One display is live at a time — the local screen or a Cast receiver, never both. There is no control for that today: the header button opens and closes the local screen, and receivers are picked from the Chromecast plugin's own table. Four ways to present the choice, in KHost's own classes.

01 One list, radio selection Recommended

Every display in one place, whichever provider found it, with the live one marked. The radio shape carries the rule by itself — picking one plainly unpicks the other, so "one at a time" needs no sentence to explain it.

The badge is the honest part: a host learns before choosing that a television cannot draw the words and the host will burn them in instead. Searching sits at the bottom because a sweep is rare and deliberate, not the first thing you reach for.

02 Header button says what is live

Not an alternative so much as the way into one. The button already reports whether anything is on; it becomes the display's name, and the press opens the picker rather than toggling the local screen.

Worth having whichever list wins — a host glances at the header to answer "where is this going?" without opening anything.

Off is a position, not a separate button.

03 Segmented control Rejected

Compact, and "off" reads as just another position. Fine with two displays.

It stops working the moment a venue has three receivers — the names truncate and the control overflows a panel that can be 180px wide. It also has nowhere to put the badge that says a device cannot draw the words, which is the thing a host most needs to know.

04 The two states that decide it

Before anything is up, the local screen is still offered as a row rather than a button, so the list is the one place displays live. The empty line says what is true — nothing has been found — rather than implying a failure.

Switching mid-song asks first, because it is not free: the new display gets its own stream and starts from where the song is, which the room hears as a gap. A host who meant it gets a short wait; a host who mis-clicked gets their show back. Switching while nothing is playing needs no prompt at all.
